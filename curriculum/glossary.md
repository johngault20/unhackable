# Glossary

Plain-language definitions for terms used in the curriculum. These are learning aids, not exhaustive technical specifications.

- **Authentication:** Checking that a person or service is who it claims to be, usually through a password, key, or other factor.
- **Availability:** The ability to use a system or data when needed. Ransomware can harm availability by making files inaccessible.
- **Backup:** A separate copy of important data that can be used to recover after deletion, device failure, or compromise. A backup should be tested.
- **Credential:** Information used to prove identity, such as a username and password, private key, or one-time code.
- **Defense in depth:** Using multiple protective layers so that one failure does not automatically expose everything.
- **Exfiltration:** Unauthorized removal or transfer of information from a system.
- **Firewall:** A control that allows or restricts network connections according to rules. A local firewall status alone does not establish whether a service is reachable from the internet.
- **Incident:** An event that affects or may affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of systems or data.
- **Indicator:** A clue that may be relevant to an investigation, but needs context and verification. An indicator is not automatically proof.
- **Least privilege:** Giving an account or program only the permissions it needs to perform its task.
- **MFA (multifactor authentication):** Requiring more than one type of proof to sign in. MFA can reduce risk but does not eliminate it.
- **Persistence:** A broad term for methods used to maintain access across sessions or restarts. This curriculum discusses it only from a defensive perspective.
- **Phishing:** Deceptive communication designed to persuade someone to reveal information, approve access, or run something unsafe.
- **Privilege:** The permissions an account or process has. Administrative privilege allows more extensive system changes.
- **Risk-review priority:** A limited, transparent prompt for human review, not a validated security score or verdict.
- **Supply-chain compromise:** A compromise involving software, a vendor, or a dependency trusted by downstream users.
- **Threat intelligence:** Information about threats, incidents, or vulnerabilities. It can be incomplete or irrelevant to a particular device.
- **Unknown:** A deliberate result when evidence is missing or unclear; it must not be interpreted as safe.
